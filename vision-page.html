Enhancing WordPress's Mission: Democratizing Publishing → Democratizing Digital Sovereignty

"Democratizing publishing" gave everyone a voice. The next step: giving everyone ownership of their digital life.

Playground for Users

We have focused on Playground as a developer and showcase tool—a place to spin up a fresh WordPress and try something. But with persistence, it becomes something more: a free WordPress for everyone. A place to get you hooked.

Playground being ephemeral is surprising to users but powerful for developers. By providing another instance of Playground but persistent, we can give users the experience they know from a regular WordPress. Just without creating an account, signing up for hosting, or having to choose a domain.

WordPress as a useful private tool

The idea of using WordPress for yourself isn't new. But it hasn't caught on:

  1. Many people don't even have a personal WordPress (anymore).
  2. If they have one, they don't want to risk it by installing plugins for that use case.
  3. If they try it on a test blog, it will always remain a test.

Because this WordPress lives only in your browser, it's inherently private. No one else can access it. No account needed, no server to secure, no data leaving your machine.

This isn't a limitation—it's a feature. A space where you can experiment freely, store private thoughts, organize your digital life, without worrying about who might see it.

The Chicken-and-Egg Problem

There's a reason WordPress isn't used more for personal use:

  1. Everybody thinks WordPress = Publishing, and rightfully so. It is great at that.
  2. Few people seek to use it for purposes that are not publishing → No demand for plugins like that.
  3. With no supply of personal plugins, WordPress cannot shine at that.

A Persistent Playground breaks this cycle by reducing the barrier of entry. We can reduce the friction to experience a personally useful WordPress by providing a selection of plugins that make it a great personal WordPress, which we could then call apps.

But we also need to build the apps. Both to get users, and to inspire developers what they can do.

Also, the sandboxed nature of Playground makes it a perfect place for people to interact with AI: It can modify your WordPress. You can ask it to modify plugins that don't quite do what you want. It can build new plugins from scratch based on what you describe, even blocks given the constraints of the environment.

WordPress.org as an App Store

The WordPress Plugin directory is great for discovering and browsing plugins. But it's bad at actually installing the plugin, mostly because it doesn't know your WordPress address. If we provide one canonical WordPress that everyone can use, we can add a "Install to your Playground" button. One click—it's in your WordPress. Discovery and installation become one step.

Students can use it to save notes. It can be a private photo store. Maybe it's where you talk to LLMs and organize those conversations. The personal use cases emerge when the barrier disappears.

Why Now

AI needs personal data. AI tools work best with your own data—your notes, your contacts, your history. People are realizing they need a place to store this, under their control. WordPress can be that place.

Platform fatigue is real. People are tired of platforms that monetize their attention and lock in their data. They want spaces that are theirs. A WordPress in your browser, with no account and no tracking, is the antidote.

The technology is ready. Playground has matured. OPFS, persistence, the infrastructure—it all works now. We can deliver on this vision today.

A Stepping Stone to A Hosted WordPress

Some people need a free WordPress for themselves. They could stay in the persistent Playground.

But when you're ready for more—multiple devices, sharing publicly, collaboration—you migrate to hosted WordPress.

Everything you built comes with you. Playground is the gateway, hosted WordPress is the destination. And maybe you'll then start blogging after all.
